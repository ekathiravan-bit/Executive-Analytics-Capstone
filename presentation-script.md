# Three-minute presentation script

Use this to record the walkthrough (about 3 minutes at a steady pace). Open the deck, then the dashboard from Task 2 for the last ten seconds if you want to show where the numbers come from.

| Slide | Time | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cover | 0:00 | "Our question is practical: with limited time, which customers do we contact first to prevent churn that could have been avoided? This briefing gives the short answer, five findings, what it could be worth, and how sure we are." |
| 2. The short answer | 0:15 | "Contact month-to-month customers in their first year as soon as they raise a third support ticket. All seven month-to-month customers in this sample churned, and none of the eight on longer contracts did. If outreach saves 10 to 30 percent of would-be leavers, that is worth 41 to 123 a month. The sample is only 15 customers, so this is a lead to test." |
| 3. Finding 1, contract | 0:35 | "Seven of seven month-to-month customers left. Zero of eight on one-year or two-year contracts did. Contract type may show how committed someone already was, so this tells us who leaves, not why." |
| 4. Finding 2, tenure | 0:50 | "Churn is early. Six of the seven who left did so within a year, and nobody lasted past 14 months. Churned customers averaged 7 months of tenure, against 29 for customers who stayed." |
| 5. Finding 3, tickets | 1:05 | "Every customer who left had raised three or more tickets, averaging 4.3. No customer who stayed had more than two. Tickets may be a symptom of unhappiness, but they are a signal we can see early." |
| 6. Finding 4, revenue | 1:20 | "We lost 46.7 percent of customers but 32.3 percent of monthly revenue, because the customers who left pay less. The four two-year customers bring in 47 percent of revenue and none left, so protect them while targeting the early, lower-value customers." |
| 7. Finding 5, risk score | 1:40 | "A simple score from contract, tenure and tickets puts all seven customers who left in the high-risk tier and nobody else. That ranks who to contact first. But I set the weights after seeing this data, so it shows fit, not proven prediction." |
| 8. Expected impact | 2:00 | "If outreach keeps 10 to 30 percent of customers who would leave, we keep 41 to 123 a month, or 492 to 1,476 a year. That 10 to 30 percent is my planning assumption, not a measured result." |
| 9. Uncertainty | 2:20 | "What could change this: the sample is small, the weights are fitted to the same data, these are correlations not causes, and we have no usage or billing data. The data itself was clean." |
| 10. Decision | 2:40 | "The decision: approve a 90-day pilot. Contact month-to-month customers in their first year once they raise a third ticket, and compare churn against a similar group we do not contact. Thank you." |

Tips: keep your pace calm, pause on the three numbers (7 of 7, 41 to 123, 15 customers), and do not add claims that are not on the slides.
